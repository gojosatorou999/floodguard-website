    01 — The flood problem

    Flood risk is changing. The way we assess it hasn't.

    Flood risk is no longer defined by what happened in the past. Changing rainfall patterns, expanding exposure and evolving climate conditions are reshaping where and how losses occur. Yet many important decisions still depend on fragmented, historical or coarse risk information.

    The challenge isn't a lack of data. It's turning the right data into usable intelligence when a decision needs to be made.

    05 — Decision support

    From flood risk to actionable insight.

    Each layer resolves the one beneath it. Nothing is inferred from a single source, and every layer stays inspectable on its own.

    01

    Hazard signals

    Observed rainfall, river levels and recorded flood events, held as one continuous series from 1951.

    02

    Terrain & drainage

    03

    Exposure

    People, assets and land use in the path of the water, resolved from district down to site.

    04

    Hazard × exposure × vulnerability, combined into a single comparable measure rather than four separate ones.

    06 — Platform

    The intelligence behind FloodGuard

    Data in. Intelligence out.

    Five parts, each inspectable on its own. Nothing here is a black box — every number we publish can be traced back to the data and the method that produced it.

    Data

    Observed climate, satellite and remote sensing, terrain, hydrological records and climate projections, held as one continuous series rather than a set of disconnected extracts.

    • Observed rainfall and river levels
    • Satellite and remote sensing
    • Terrain, drainage and land use
    • Climate projections

    Technology

    Statistical, machine-learning and climate-modelling approaches, applied where each is appropriate rather than everywhere at once.

    • Hydrological simulation
    • Machine learning where it earns its place
    • Reproducible model runs

    Methodology

    How hazard, exposure and vulnerability are combined, written down and published — so a result can be argued with rather than taken on trust.

    • Documented assumptions
    • Stated uncertainty
    • Versioned method notes

    Validation

    Model output checked against recorded events, and the checks reported alongside the result rather than kept separately.

    • Back-testing against observed floods
    • Independent review of method
    • Published performance notes

    API

    Maps, reports, data services and endpoints that drop into the workflows you already run, instead of asking you to move into ours.

    • REST endpoints for risk and exposure
    • Bulk data delivery
    • Tiles for your own map stack
