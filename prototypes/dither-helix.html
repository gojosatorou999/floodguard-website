Why this version is quiet

Dither is cartography, not nostalgia

Ordered Bayer dithering is what quantized raster geospatial data actually looks like. Used here it reads as instrument output rather than a retro web effect — which is the whole difference between "technical" and "flashy" for a procurement audience.

Dissolve carries information

Breakdown is mapped to focus: the centered card resolves to clean pixels, peripheral cards decay into their grid. The effect states which deliverable you're looking at instead of just decorating the scene.

The helix is a specimen case

Contained in a bordered panel beside the headline, not a full-bleed background. Shallow arc, 34° between cards, small vertical rise. It reads as a product showcase, and the copy still leads.

The cursor trail paints data, not colour

Moving across a card dissolves it locally into its pixel grid, healing behind you. It's the same "this is data, not a photograph" argument as the dissolve — a rainbow smear would have said nothing.

Discrete advance, never a spin

Cards step on a timer with eased transitions rather than rotating continuously. There's a real pause control — WCAG 2.2.2 requires auto-moving content be pausable, and public-sector procurement checks for it.

It degrades to a still

Under prefers-reduced-motion the auto-advance stops, entry becomes a short fade, and the trail switches off. Card text is real DOM throughout, so it survives with no canvas at all.
